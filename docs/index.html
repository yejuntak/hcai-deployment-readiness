Research motivation

Does a finished screen change what we examine?

A polished prototype can show an interface without explaining its requirements, dependencies or recovery paths. The research asks how visual fidelity affects readiness judgments and attention to structural issues.

H.A.R.D. provides a structured review of the work behind the interface. It connects the intended outcome to the artifact, the available evidence and the people responsible for the next step.

The proposed study has not established an effect. This protocol is a review method, not an image filter or a claim that polished work is defective.

In practice

Review the work behind the interface.

  1. Keep the original artifact.

    Review the actual design or code revision. Do not blur labels or remove information people need.

  2. Map the structure when useful.

    In a separate view, map the task, choices, dependencies and recovery paths. Name who acts and mark what is still unknown.

  3. Check requirements and evidence.

    Check the artifact against its requirements and evidence. A drawing cannot supply a missing test, baseline or human decision.

A separate structure view is optional review guidance, not an extra gate. Keep it separate from the proposed study design.

Preview the six questions
  1. What happens today?

    Record the steps, time, failures and rework.

  2. What needs to improve, for whom?

    Tie the requirement to an actual need.

  3. What happens when something goes wrong?

    Define recovery and who may act.

  4. What demonstrates the behavior?

    Trace the requirement to the artifact and its check.

  5. What work remains for people?

    Count review, correction and escalation.

  6. Is there enough evidence to fund the next step?

    Apply the risk depth and name an owner.

Stop at a failed or missing gate. A good answer elsewhere cannot cancel it out. Without a measurable baseline, ROI stays indeterminate.

Look across the work

Some questions live outside the interface.

Use these four views to bring the right people into the review. They are discussion aids, not extra gates or separate scores.

01

Experience and information

Can people find their way?

Check labels, information hierarchy and competing choices against the task people need to complete.

02

Workflow and architecture

Do the handoffs work?

Follow the dependencies and state changes, including what happens when a step fails.

03

Implementation and evidence

What has been checked?

Distinguish working behavior from simulation. Link each important requirement to a check of the exact artifact.

04

People and operation

Who handles the remaining work?

Name the people responsible for review, correction and support. Include their time when estimating savings.

What you leave with

01 / Decision

A recommendation

Proceed to engineering, revise, or gather missing evidence.

02 / Evidence

A reason

The evidence checked and the questions still open.

03 / Responsibility

A next action

A named owner, a defined task and a resource limit.

The owner authorizes engineering separately; deployment requires separate evaluation.

Research question and study boundaries

The proposed study varies visual fidelity while holding content, behavior and defects constant. It does not establish that all AI-built work skips design steps. A separate structure view is a practice aid, not an unplanned intervention for that study.

Evidence and limitations

This is a Public Preview. Practitioner correspondence informed its refinement, but it is not controlled empirical validation. Software tests check implementation behavior. Usability, time savings and effectiveness remain unvalidated.
