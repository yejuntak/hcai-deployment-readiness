# What this looks like in practice

Protocol 0.1-rc.4-candidate.2 · All examples below are constructed, not external pilot results.

## 1. An owner wants to automate intake

The proposed AI intake screen is polished. The owner cannot say how many requests arrive, how long staff spend, or where cases get stuck.

**Useful outcome:** INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE at G1; ROI indeterminate. Observe a recent case and record volume before debating the proposed solution. This protects the decision from an attractive but ungrounded savings story.

## 2. A designer presents a convincing booking prototype

The normal flow works in the demonstration. After payment failure, the user's entered details disappear and no recovery owner is defined.

**Useful outcome:** REVISE at G3. Define and validate the recovery path. A realistic appearance does not demonstrate complete behavior. The exact revised artifact must link to that validation.

## 3. An agent edits code after the tests pass

The test report is green, but its recorded artifact digest belongs to an earlier revision. The new code changes the permission check.

**Useful outcome:** REVISE at G4. Revalidate affected requirements against the exact new revision. More screenshots cannot repair a stale test link.

## 4. A helpful agent starts acting on behalf of a person

A prototype can draft and send customer messages, but nobody has specified approval, cancellation, escalation, or who can revoke its authority.

**Useful outcome:** REVISE or INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE at G3, depending on whether the omission is observed or unknown. Bound permitted actions, data use, human approval and recovery. Higher impact or difficult reversal requires FULL; a short checklist is not enough.

## 5. AI wrote the interface, but does not run the service

A team used an AI coding assistant to build a conventional internal form. There are no AI-generated outputs during operation.

**Useful outcome:** record ai_role as artifact_creation. Assess operational review burden honestly: relevant AI-output review can be zero with a reason, while normal support, error correction, and manual work may remain. Do not invent a runtime AI risk just because AI helped write code.

## 6. Gross savings disappear during review

A fictional case saves 15 minutes before oversight. Checking takes 8 minutes, corrections 5, and escalation/rework 2. Net labor savings are zero before any recurring service cost.

**Useful outcome:** show gross 15 and net 0 separately. Do not preserve the original inflated ROI. If the team still wants to invest for another reason, record the explicit rationale; the tool does not authorize that choice.

## 7. A researcher tests whether polish changes defect detection

Participants judge whether each requirement has a defect and how confident they are. AI authorship is held constant; fidelity varies.

**Useful outcome:** a separate study-review record, not an engineering recommendation. Keep ground-truth answers outside the participant record. Perceived readiness is not actual readiness. Guided criteria may change the experimental task, so do not introduce them without the study design specifying that intervention.

## What these examples do not establish

They illustrate intended use and testable boundaries. They do not establish reduced defects, improved ROI, universal usability, or the causal effects of AI coding. Those require appropriate empirical work.
