# Comparative validation plan

Status: proposed. No participants, data, institutional determination or effectiveness results are represented.

## Primary question
Does the locked-judgment and adjudication workflow reduce false-ready acceptance of criterion-nonready interface prototypes beyond an equally detailed requirements and recovery checklist under the same time and training budget?

## Comparator and assignment
Give both conditions the same task, mandatory requirements, recovery checks and artifact access. Compare that strong checklist baseline with the additional protocol procedure. Pre-specify assignment, timing, exclusions, stopping rule and analysis before data collection. Balance evaluator expertise and prior exposure. Use different matched cases across conditions or counterbalanced assignment to limit carryover. Report time and adjudication effort so extra labor is visible.

## Case construction
Develop independently reviewed ready and nonready cases. For a visual-fidelity hypothesis, cross presentation polish (lower/higher) with functional completeness (ready/nonready) while holding task content and interactions constant within each completeness condition. Verify the intended polish manipulation separately. Preserve source and rendered versions. Treat AI-generated provenance as a separate documented population variable; do not infer a causal AI effect from labels or appearance. The current text-only SR-01 is training material and does not implement this design.

## Reference and blinding
Have at least two qualified reviewers independently assess seeded defect observability, mandatory-check status and severity before adjudication. Preserve disagreements and final rationale. Keep evaluators blind to outcomes and other evaluators' findings. Use new test cases and record prior knowledge of public materials. Record all findings including unsupported suggestions, not only true positives.

## Outcomes
Pre-specify the primary criterion-nonready false-ready rate with its original denominator. Always report abstention, decision coverage, decisive false-ready rate, false holds on ready cases, missingness and unknown criterion status. Report reference recall, critical-defect detection, unsupported-finding burden, omission recognition, per-session signed and absolute expected-recall gaps, time and adjudication disagreement as separate outcomes. Do not reward a method that refuses or abstains on every case.

## Analysis and limits
The 6-12 practitioner feasibility target is not powered to establish superiority. Use it to check comprehension, duration, missingness and coding reliability. For a later effectiveness study, justify sample size with the planned clustered design and outcome variance. Repeated cases per evaluator and repeated evaluators per case require analysis that respects both sources of dependence. Preserve raw per-instance counts and negative results. Avoid a pooled binomial interval that treats dependent judgments as independent.

## Thirty-day deliverables
Week 1: technical corrections and external desk-review records. Week 2: independently reviewed cases, study plan and applicable institutional determination. Week 3: feasible permitted sessions with locked raw records. Week 4: descriptive feasibility report, limitations and versioned changes. If review or recruitment does not occur, report that status instead of results. No minimum improvement is promised.
